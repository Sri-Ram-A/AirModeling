What is a Gaussian Plume?

When a chimney releases pollutants, the wind carries them downwind while atmospheric turbulence spreads them outward. The resulting concentration profile follows a Gaussian (bell-curve) shape in both crosswind and vertical directions.

$$C(x,y,z=0) = \frac{Q}{\pi u \sigma_y \sigma_z} \exp\!\left(-\frac{y^2}{2\sigma_y^2}\right)\exp\!\left(-\frac{H^2}{2\sigma_z^2}\right)$$
The Transport Matrix Idea

With N monitoring stations, each acting as both source and receptor, we build an N×N matrix T where entry Tij answers:

"If source j emits 1 g/s, what concentration (µg/m³) reaches receptor i?"
$$\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{T} \cdot \mathbf{Q}$$ C ∈ ℝᴺ measured concentrations T ∈ ℝᴺˣᴺ transport matrix Q ∈ ℝᴺ unknown emission rates
Why This Matters
Air quality inversion — given measured C and known T, solve for Q (who is polluting?)
Forecasting — given emission Q and weather T, predict concentrations C at any sensor
Regulatory compliance — identify which sources violate air quality standards
