Stability Class & Dispersion σ

The atmosphere's turbulence level controls how fast pollutants spread. Pasquill-Gifford (PG) stability classes A–F parameterize this with empirical power-law coefficients σ_y and σ_z.

Determining the Class
// Simplified Pasquill-Gifford lookup if daytime: u < 2 m/s, SR > 600 W/m² → A (very unstable) u < 3 m/s, SR > 300 W/m² → B (unstable) u < 5 m/s, SR > 50 W/m² → C (sl. unstable) else → D (neutral) if nighttime: u < 2 m/s → F (stable) u < 3 m/s → E (sl. stable) else → D (neutral)

SR = solar radiation [W/m²]. Daytime unstable conditions (A/B) produce large σ values — wide, diluted plumes. Stable night conditions (E/F) keep plumes narrow and concentrated.

Physical meaning of σ:
σ_y and σ_z are the standard deviations of the Gaussian concentration profile in the crosswind and vertical directions. 68% of the pollutant mass sits within ±1σ, 95% within ±2σ of the plume centreline.
