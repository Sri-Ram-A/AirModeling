Bearing Formula — Derivation
The full formula:
$$\theta = \operatorname{atan2}\!\left(\sin(\Delta\lambda)\cos(\varphi_2),\;\cos(\varphi_1)\sin(\varphi_2)-\sin(\varphi_1)\cos(\varphi_2)\cos(\Delta\lambda)\right)$$ $$\theta_{deg} = \left(\theta \cdot \frac{180}{\pi}\right) \bmod 360°$$
y component (numerator):
y = sin(Δλ) · cos(φ₂) Measures the East-West component of displacement. Large y → more eastward/westward.
x component (denominator):
x = cos(φ₁)·sin(φ₂) − sin(φ₁)·cos(φ₂)·cos(Δλ) Measures the North-South component of displacement.
Why atan2 instead of atan?
atan2(y, x) knows which quadrant the angle is in because it receives both y and x separately. Plain atan(y/x) would lose the sign information and produce angles only in [−90°, +90°]. atan2 gives the full [0°, 360°] range needed for compass bearings.

Why bearing matters for Tij:
The bearing θ tells us the direction from source to receptor in geographic (compass) space. We then compare it to the wind direction to find out if the receptor is downwind, upwind, or crosswind — which determines the entire plume calculation.
